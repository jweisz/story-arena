# rits:granite-3-3-8b-instruct
My future vision of how AI will reshape the practice of software engineering is two-fold. First, AI will gradually take over the lower-level details of writing and testing code, enabling people who currently toil over the particularities of coding to focus on higher-value, higher-level tasks requiring more creativity, such as determining what code to write. In this way, software engineering will become more like what product management is today: defining specifications for functionality and feature specifications, and leaving the implementation to the AI. However, the second impact will come from the other direction where people who do not have coding skills are able to produce robust software in ways that would currently require them to hire a team of developers. In this way, "software engineering" becomes a more democratized field that no longer requires high levels of specialized training to perform. Some people will still find value in learning to code and understanding lower-level abstractions, akin to how most people today know how to compose stories in English without having formally majored in English in college. In the longer term, people will no longer be differented by their individual skill in writing code, nor by their ability to build high-performing teams of (human) software engineers. Rather, entrepreneureal skills will be more of a determinant for one's success. People who can identify important problems to solve for which other people are willing to pay money will be economically successful. By analogy, in the world where anyone can build a hammer, the hammer is no longer a differentiating tool (it becomes commodity), and even the process of building the hammer is no longer a differentiator, so the differentiator comes down to how that hammer is used. People who are really good at using hammers to build other things will be successful; people who aren't good at figuring out creative ways to use a hammer won't be successful. So it will be with coding.
